# Neutron Decay as Boundary Relaxation of a Local Twist Defect

## 1. Neutron as an Almost‑Spherical Boundary with a Local Twist
We model the neutron as a nearly spherical boundary mode with a small, localized
directional phase defect. Its boundary state can be written as

$$
\Psi_n = \Psi_{\text{sph}} + \delta\Psi_{\text{twist}},
$$

where  
- $\Psi_{\text{sph}}$ is the closed, curvature‑supported spherical harmonic, and  
- $\delta\Psi_{\text{twist}}$ is a local, non‑closed, directional twist that a spherical
boundary cannot support (a boundary‑harmonic analogue of the “no‑hair” constraint).

This defect stores tension because a sphere cannot sustain a unidirectional twist.

---

## 2. Geometric Decomposition of the Defect
The twist defect naturally decomposes into two orthogonal components:

$$
\delta\Psi_{\text{twist}}
= \delta\Psi_{\text{closed}} + \delta\Psi_{\text{open}}.
$$

- $\delta\Psi_{\text{closed}}$ is curvature‑compatible and can be absorbed into a spherical harmonic.  
- $\delta\Psi_{\text{open}}$ is a directional, propagating twist that cannot remain on a closed surface.

This decomposition is enforced by boundary geometry.

---

## 3. Electron Formation: Closed Circulation Component
The closed component becomes a stable spherical harmonic:

$$
\Psi_e = \delta\Psi_{\text{closed}}.
$$

This mode supports:
- electric charge (closed twist),
- magnetic moment (rotational projection),
- self‑induction energy (a major contribution to electron mass).

Thus the electron emerges as the curvature‑compatible part of the neutron’s defect.

---

## 4. Neutrino Formation: Directional Chiral Component
The open component becomes a linear, long‑cycle wavelet:

$$
\Psi_\nu = \delta\Psi_{\text{open}}.
$$

This mode:
- carries the directional twist,
- propagates away from the boundary,
- has its handedness selected by the chiral term in the update operator.

Only the twist orientation that *reduces* the neutron’s local tension produces a stable escaping mode; the opposite orientation collapses.

---

## 5. Proton as the Cleaned‑Up Spherical Remainder
After shedding both components of the defect, the remaining boundary becomes a
perfectly closed spherical harmonic:

$$
\Psi_p = \Psi_{\text{sph}}.
$$

This mode is maximally stable, with no residual twist or chirality.

---

## 6. Structural Interpretation of Neutron Decay
Neutron decay is not an arbitrary weak‑interaction process.  
It is the **geometric relaxation** of a boundary that contains a twist defect it cannot support:

$$
\Psi_n \;\longrightarrow\; \Psi_p + \Psi_e + \Psi_\nu.
$$

- $\Psi_e$ carries the closed circulation component.  
- $\Psi_\nu$ carries the directional chiral component.  
- $\Psi_p$ is the defect‑free spherical remainder.

The decay is therefore a **necessary boundary‑relaxation channel**, not a contingent interaction.

## 7. Selection of Neutrino Handedness by the Chiral Update Operator

The directional component of the neutron’s twist defect, $\delta\Psi_{\text{open}}$, can in
principle unwind in two orientations (left‑ or right‑handed). In a parity‑symmetric
update rule, both orientations would be dynamically equivalent. However, the boundary
update operator contains a small intrinsic chiral term,

$$
\mathcal{U}
= \mathcal{U}_0 \;+\; \varepsilon\,\mathcal{U}_{\chi},
$$

where  
- $\mathcal{U}_0$ is the parity‑symmetric part of the propagation rule,  
- $\mathcal{U}_{\chi}$ is a chiral (parity‑odd) contribution, and  
- $\varepsilon \ll 1$ sets the strength of the chiral bias.

This term breaks the degeneracy between the two possible unwindings of the defect.

## 7. Selection of Neutrino Handedness by the Chiral Update Operator

- $\varepsilon \ll 1$ sets the strength of the chiral bias.

This term breaks the degeneracy between the two possible unwindings of the defect.

---

### 7.1. Energy Descent and the Preferred Unwinding Direction
The local twist defect carries stored boundary tension. Under the update operator,
the rate of tension relaxation is

$$
\frac{dE}{dt}
= \left\langle \delta\Psi_{\text{open}},\;
\mathcal{U}\,\delta\Psi_{\text{open}} \right\rangle.
$$

Because $\mathcal{U}_{\chi}$ is parity‑odd, the two twist orientations satisfy

$$
\left\langle \delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(+)} ,\;
\mathcal{U}_{\chi}\,\delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(+)} \right\rangle
= -\left\langle \delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(-)} ,\;
\mathcal{U}_{\chi}\,\delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(-)} \right\rangle.
$$

Thus one orientation is *downhill* in tension (faster relaxation), while the opposite
orientation is *uphill* (increasing tension or failing to relax).

Only the downhill orientation produces a stable escaping mode.

---

### 7.2. Emergence of Neutrino Handedness
The escaping neutrino wavelet is therefore

$$
\Psi_\nu
= \delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(\text{downhill})},
$$

while the opposite orientation is dynamically suppressed:

$$
\delta\Psi_{\text{open}}^{(\text{uphill})} \;\longrightarrow\; 0.
$$

This mechanism selects a unique helicity for the neutrino. The same chiral term
$\mathcal{U}_{\chi}$ appears macroscopically as CP‑violating behavior in weak processes,
but here it is seen as a microscopic geometric bias in boundary relaxation.

### 7.3. Structural Interpretation
Neutrino handedness is therefore not an imposed rule but a consequence of:

1. a local twist defect on a spherical boundary (neutron),  
2. the geometric impossibility of supporting such a twist on a closed surface, and  
3. a small chiral bias in the update operator that determines the downhill
   relaxation path.

The neutrino is the directional component of the defect following the unique
energy‑descending channel permitted by $\mathcal{U}_{\chi}$.
