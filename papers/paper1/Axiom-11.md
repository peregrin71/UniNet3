### AXIOM‑11  
Spectral smoothness — a friendly way to think about generated updates

A substrate with a single, homogeneous update rule (Axiom‑4) has a discrete time‑translation symmetry:  
each tick applies the same transformation.  
For such a reversible, local rule to produce coherent large‑scale behavior, repeated updates must form a well‑behaved group rather than a sequence of unrelated unitaries.  
This requires the update operator to admit a local generator.

Formally, the global update operator $U$ must satisfy  
$$
U = e^{-iH\,\Delta t},
$$  
for some Hermitian $H$ acting only on bounded‑diameter neighborhoods.

This axiom does not impose continuity on the substrate.  
It states that locality, reversibility, and update homogeneity together require the evolution to be generated by a local Hermitian operator if the system is to support stable dispersion, persistent excitations, and a coherent continuum limit.  
Without a generator, the discrete time‑translation symmetry implied by Axiom‑4 cannot be promoted to a stable spectral structure, and long‑lived modes (Axiom‑10) cannot exist.

### AXIOM‑11  
Spectral smoothness — a friendly way to think about generated updates

A substrate that applies the same local, reversible update rule at every tick (Axiom‑4) has a discrete time‑translation symmetry: each step is structurally identical to the next.  
For such a system, locality and information preservation imply that repeated updates cannot behave like unrelated moves.  
If they did, large‑scale patterns would fragment, long‑wavelength structures would not persist, and no coherent continuum behaviour could emerge.

To avoid this, the update must belong to a smoothly generated family of transformations.  
Formally, there must exist a local operator $G$, acting only on bounded‑diameter neighborhoods, such that the global update $U$ can be written as  
$$
U = e^{-i\,G\,\Delta t}.
$$

This axiom does not impose continuity on the substrate.  
It states that a homogeneous, reversible, local update rule can only support durable, scalable behaviour if its evolution is generated by a local operator.  
Without such a generator, the discrete time‑translation symmetry implied by Axiom‑4 cannot produce a stable spectral structure, and long‑lived excitations (Axiom‑10) cannot exist.
