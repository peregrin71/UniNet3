# LEMMA-TIME-02 — Conflation of Time Types and the Problem of Time

**Status:** conceptual lemma (explanatory)

## Statement

When distinct notions of time—external evolution parameter (QM), foliation-dependent coordinate time (GR), and proper time along worldlines—are implicitly treated as if they were the same type of object, apparent contradictions arise in attempts to combine quantum mechanics and general relativity.  
This conflation underlies what is commonly referred to as the “problem of time” in quantum gravity.  
UniNet avoids this issue by not postulating any primitive time variable and by keeping substrate update order, causal structure, and emergent time notions explicitly distinct.

## Interpretation (reader-facing)

In quantum mechanics, the time parameter in the Schrödinger equation is external and fixed.  
In general relativity, time is encoded in the spacetime metric and depends on a choice of foliation.  
Operationally, clocks measure proper time along specific worldlines.  
Treating these three as if they were the same “time” leads to tensions such as:

- a Hamiltonian that both generates evolution (QM) and vanishes as a constraint (GR),  
- ambiguity about what “evolution in time” means in a diffeomorphism-invariant theory,  
- and confusion about how to compare quantum states defined on different spacelike slices.

From UniNet’s perspective, these tensions are not fundamental paradoxes but artifacts of a type conflation: different time concepts being identified as one.  
By deriving temporal structure from update order and causal propagation on the substrate, and only then introducing emergent time notions within witness families, UniNet sidesteps the traditional “problem of time” rather than solving it within its original assumptions.
