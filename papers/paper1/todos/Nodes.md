A note on buffering (an open hinge)
One detail is deliberately left open at this stage: what exactly happens to information while it is buffered inside a node. When local update rules and finite propagation speed prevent immediate transfer, information may accumulate temporarily. Whether that buffered content continues to evolve internally or simply waits to be released is not fixed here. Both behaviors are consistent with the core structure: locality still enforces delay, conservation still holds once boundary bookkeeping is included, and the global causal order remains unchanged.
This choice does not affect the cut structure or the resulting delays — those are already guaranteed by graph locality. Instead, it marks a modeling hinge that becomes relevant only when one asks how much internal history a bottleneck can hide, and how much reconstructability is lost before information re‑enters the graph. The framework keeps this hinge open on purpose, allowing different dynamical regimes to be explored later without altering the conceptual spine.
