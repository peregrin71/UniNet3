- **Admissibility:**
   - $\ell_e = 1$ for all $e$ (unit edge length by definition)

## 4. Main Theorem: Delay-to-Effective-Length Bridge

**Theorem (Conditional):**

If the above axioms and admissibility conditions hold, then for each edge $e$ and tick $n$,
$$
c^*_e(n) = 1 + \lambda_e(n),
$$
where
$$
\lambda_e(n) = f(\rho_e(n)), \quad f(0) = 0, \; f(1) \geq 0.
$$
Here $f$ is a non-decreasing function of the buffering density, determined by the model. The effective geometry is generated by a uniform constitutive rule depending only on $\rho$.
### C. Proof: Triangle Inequality for Effective Edge Lengths

**Statement:** For any triangle formed by three nodes $u, v, w$ with edges $e_1 = (u,v)$, $e_2 = (v,w)$, $e_3 = (w,u)$, the effective edge lengths $c^*_{e_i}(n)$ satisfy the triangle inequalities:
$$
c^*_{e_1}(n) + c^*_{e_2}(n) \geq c^*_{e_3}(n), \quad \text{and cyclic permutations.}
$$

**Proof:**
By construction, $c^*_{e}(n) = 1 + \lambda_e(n)$ with $\lambda_e(n) \geq 0$ for all $e$. Thus, for any triangle:
$$
c^*_{e_1} + c^*_{e_2} = (1 + \lambda_{e_1}) + (1 + \lambda_{e_2}) = 2 + \lambda_{e_1} + \lambda_{e_2} \geq 1 + \lambda_{e_3} = c^*_{e_3},
$$
since $\lambda_{e_i} \geq 0$ for all $i$. The same argument holds for all cyclic permutations. Therefore, the triangle inequalities are always satisfied for the effective edge lengths in this model.

### Definition 5.1 (Fixed Microscopic Edge Lengths)

Let $G=(V,E)$ be finite, connected. Each microscopic edge has fixed length:
$$
\ell_e>0,\quad e\in E.
$$
Uniform case: $\ell_e=\ell_0$.

Status: `proved` (definition).

### Definition 5.2 (State-Derived Load Observables)

At tick $n$, define edge-local observables from microscopic state and flux bookkeeping:
$$
\rho_e(n)\in[0,1],\quad |J_e(n)|\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}.
$$

Status: `proved` (definition + existing framework symbols).

### Definition 5.3 (Constitutive Slowdown Law)

Assume a constitutive function
$$
\phi:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\to[1,\infty)
$$
with monotonicity constraints
$$
\partial_{\rho}\phi\ge0,\qquad \partial_{K}\phi\ge0.
$$
$K$ is a curvature-effort channel.

Status: `postulate` (compatible with `R9`).

### Definition 5.3A (Local Observable Tuple)

For each edge $e$ at tick $n$, define the local observable tuple
$$
\mathcal{O}_e(n):=\big(\ell\text{-data near }e,\ \rho\text{-data near }e,\ |J|\text{-data near }e,\ K\text{-data near }e\big).
$$

Status: `proved` (definition).

### Definition 5.3B (Node-Uniform Constitutive Response)

Assume there exists a single constitutive map $\Lambda$ such that for every edge $e$ and tick $n$,
$$
\lambda_e(n)=\Lambda\!\big(\mathcal{O}_e(n)\big),
$$
with no explicit dependence on node or edge labels beyond the local observable tuple.
This is the edge-level constitutive homogeneity condition (AXIOM-5-compatible form).

Additionally, if two edge neighborhoods are related by a local graph automorphism that preserves the observable tuple, the response is identical:
$$
\mathcal{O}_{e'}(n)=g\cdot \mathcal{O}_e(n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda(\mathcal{O}_{e'}(n))=\Lambda(\mathcal{O}_e(n)).
$$

Status: `postulate`.

Reader note:

1. This is the precise form of "the same rules everywhere."
2. It does not require the state to be homogeneous; it requires only that the response law is homogeneous.
3. In this document, $\Lambda$ is not a second independent dynamics; it is the additive bridge form of the same local constitutive response later written through $\phi$ and $c^*$.

### Definition 5.4 (Curvature Functional and Closure Map)

Let $\mathcal{K}$ map an edge-cost field to an edge curvature proxy:
$$
K_e[c]\ge0.
$$
Define update map on cost fields at fixed tick $n$:
$$
\mathcal{T}_n(c)_e:=\ell_e\,\phi\!\left(\rho_e(n),|J_e(n)|,K_e[c]\right).
$$
A self-consistent cost field satisfies:
$$
c^*(n)=\mathcal{T}_n(c^*(n)).
$$

Status: `postulate` (closure specification).

### Definition 5.5 (Dynamic Spacetime Path Cost)

For event path
$$
\gamma=((v_0,n_0)\to(v_1,n_0+1)\to\cdots\to(v_m,n_0+m)),
$$
with traversed edges $e_k=\{v_k,v_{k+1}\}$, define
$$
\mathcal{A}[\gamma]:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1} c^*_{e_k}(n_0+k).
$$
Dynamic effective latency:
$$
\delta_{\mathrm{dyn}}((u,n_0),(v,n_1))
:=\inf_{\gamma:u\to v,\ n_1-n_0=m}\mathcal{A}[\gamma].
$$

Status: `proved` (definition).

### Definition 5.6 (Buffered Latency Increment)

For a self-consistent effective edge cost $c^*_e(n)$, define buffered latency increment:
$$
\lambda_e(n):=c^*_e(n)-\ell_e.
$$

Status: `proved` (definition).


## 6. Delay-to-Effective-Length Bridge and Admissibility Conditions

This section establishes the rigorous bridge between buffering-induced delay and effective edge length, with explicit bounds and admissibility conditions for the buffering density $\rho$. Only the minimal geometric constraints needed for physical and mathematical admissibility are imposed; deeper Regge results are referenced externally.

### 6.1 Symbol Introduction and Notation

- $e$: an edge in the network graph $G$.
- $n$: discrete tick time.
- $\ell_e$: fixed microscopic (bare) length of edge $e$, $\ell_e > 0$.
- $\rho_e(n)$: buffering density on edge $e$ at tick $n$, $0 \leq \rho_e(n) < \rho_{\max}$.
- $\phi(\rho, |J|, K)$: constitutive slowdown law, monotone non-decreasing in $\rho$ and $K$, with $\phi \geq 1$.
- $c^*_e(n)$: effective edge length (cost) at tick $n$.
- $\lambda_e(n)$: buffered latency increment, $\lambda_e(n) := c^*_e(n) - \ell_e \geq 0$.
- $K_e[c]$: curvature proxy functional (optional, for geometric closure).
- $\alpha_G$: geometric scaling parameter (optional, for Regge/curvature scaling).

### 6.2 Delay-to-Effective-Length Equivalence (Main Bridge)

For each edge $e$ and tick $n$, the effective edge length is given by:
$$
c^*_e(n) = \ell_e + \lambda_e(n),
$$
where
$$
\lambda_e(n) = \ell_e (\phi(\rho_e(n), |J_e(n)|, K_e[c^*(n)]) - 1) \geq 0.
$$
This is an exact, additive bridge: buffering/delay is mapped directly to an increment in effective edge length.

**Bounds and Admissibility for $\rho$:**
- $0 \leq \rho_e(n) < \rho_{\max}$ for all $e, n$ (positivity and upper bound; $\rho_{\max}$ set by physical/queueing constraints).
- $\phi$ must be monotone non-decreasing in $\rho$ and $K$ (ensures $\lambda_e(n) \geq 0$).
- $\ell_e > 0$ for all $e$ (ensures nondegeneracy and triangle inequalities for geometric realization).

**Minimal Geometric Admissibility:**
- The set of effective edge lengths $\{c^*_e(n)\}$ must satisfy the triangle inequalities and nondegeneracy conditions for the chosen simplicial realization (Regge admissibility).
- No further Regge dynamics or continuum limit is required for the bridge itself; these are referenced externally if needed.

### 6.3 Consequences and Physical Interpretation

- The delay-to-length mapping is exact and local: every buffering increment is realized as an increase in effective edge length.
- The effective geometry is generated by a uniform constitutive rule, not by arbitrary edge-dependent prescriptions.
- On homogeneous/isotropic branches (where $\rho_e(n)$ and $\ell_e$ are uniform), the effective geometry is isotropic.
- The only geometric constraints needed are positivity and triangle inequalities; all deeper Regge results are external.

### Theorem 8.2 (Equilateral Isotropic Seed Gives Geometric Regge Admissibility)

Assume:

1. the hypotheses of Corollary 6.0B on a chosen isotropic branch,
2. `P-C7`,
3. a baseline parameter choice $\theta_{\mathrm{bg}}$ realizing that branch.

Then, for each fixed tick on that branch, there exists a scalar $L(n)>0$ such that
$$
c^*_{\theta_{\mathrm{bg}},e}(n)=L(n)\qquad \forall e.
$$
Hence every simplex in the chosen refinement family is equilateral with positive edge length $L(n)$, and therefore nondegenerate and shape-regular. Consequently,
$$
\theta_{\mathrm{bg}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{Regge,geom}}
$$
on the chosen isotropic branch.

Status: `proved` (conditional on the chosen refinement family).

Proof:

1. Corollary 6.0B gives $c^*_e(n)=\ell_0+\lambda_{\mathrm{bg}}(n)=:L(n)$ for all edges on the isotropic branch.
2. Since $\ell_0>0$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{bg}}(n)\ge0$, we have $L(n)>0$.
3. By `P-C7`, the chosen refinement family admits nondegenerate equilateral simplices.
4. Substituting the common edge length $L(n)$ preserves equilateral nondegeneracy and shape regularity up to uniform scale.

Interpretation: the Regge bridge is not empty at the geometric level; the isotropic branch already provides a canonical seed geometry.

### Lemma 8.3 (Geometric Regge Admissibility Is an Open Condition)

Fix a finite simplicial complex. The set of edge-length assignments for which:

1. every edge length is positive,
2. every simplex satisfies the strict simplex inequalities,
3. every simplex has positive Cayley-Menger determinant bounded away from zero,
4. chosen shape-regularity lower bounds hold,

is open in the edge-length space $\mathbb{R}^{|E|}$.

Status: `proved`.

Proof:

1. Positivity and strict simplex inequalities are open conditions.
2. The Cayley-Menger determinant is a polynomial in squared edge lengths, so strict positivity is an open condition.
3. Shape-regularity lower bounds can be expressed by strict inequalities built from edge lengths and simplex volumes.
4. Finite intersections of open sets are open.

### Theorem 8.4B (Canonical Small-Coupling Regge Regime)

Assume:

1. the low-load specialization of Corollary 6.0B on the chosen isotropic branch, so the reference seed satisfies $c_e=\ell_0$,
2. the closure hypotheses of Theorem 6.2,
3. the canonical family of Definition 8.4A,
4. a geometric relative-margin $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}>0$ such that any edge field satisfying
$$
\sup_e \left|\frac{c_e-\ell_0}{\ell_0}\right|<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}
$$
around the equilateral seed length $\ell_0$ remains simplicially admissible on the chosen refinement family,
5. the contraction bound
$$
a_K L_K \ell_0<1,
$$
6. the small-deformation bound
$$
\eta_\theta<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}.
$$

Then:

1. the self-consistent cost field $c^*_\theta$ exists uniquely,
2. the bridge increment satisfies
$$
0\le \frac{\lambda_e}{\ell_0}
=a_\rho \rho_e+a_J|J_e|+a_K K_e[c^*]
\le \eta_\theta<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}},
$$
3. hence the induced edge field stays inside the admissible geometric neighborhood of the equilateral Regge seed,
4. therefore
$$
\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{Regge,geom}}.
$$

If the same margins hold uniformly across the selected refinement family, the set of all $\theta$ satisfying
$$
a_K L_K \ell_0<1,
\qquad
a_\rho \rho_{\max}+a_J J_{\max}+a_K K_{\max}<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}
$$
forms a non-empty relative-open subset of $\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^3$ contained in $\Theta_{\mathrm{Regge,geom}}$.

Status: `proved` (conditional on the stated bounds).

Proof:

1. For the linear family of Definition 8.4A, the Lipschitz constant of $\phi_\theta$ in its third argument is $L_\phi=a_K$.
2. Therefore assumption 5 is exactly the contraction condition of Theorem 6.2 on the chosen isotropic branch, so $c^*_\theta$ exists uniquely.
3. Theorem 6.0 gives
$$
\frac{\lambda_e}{\ell_0}=a_\rho \rho_e+a_J|J_e|+a_K K_e[c^*].
$$
4. Using the branch bounds from Definition 8.4A gives
$$
0\le \lambda_e/\ell_0\le \eta_\theta<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}.
$$
5. Since the reference seed satisfies $c_e=\ell_0$, we have
$$
\left|\frac{c_e-\ell_0}{\ell_0}\right|=\frac{\lambda_e}{\ell_0}<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}.
$$
6. Thus every edge length is a relative perturbation of the equilateral seed by less than $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}$, so assumption 4 places the entire edge field inside the simplicially admissible neighborhood.
7. Hence $\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{Regge,geom}}$.
8. The defining inequalities are strict, so the admissible parameter set is relative-open in the nonnegative parameter orthant.

Interpretation:

1. A safe provisional Regge-geometric regime is:
$$
0\le a_K<\frac{1}{L_K\ell_0},
\qquad
0\le a_\rho \rho_{\max}+a_J J_{\max}+a_K K_{\max}<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{geom}}.
$$
2. These are the clearest current symbolic parameter ranges for "small enough to stay Regge-admissible."
3. They are sufficient, not claimed optimal.
